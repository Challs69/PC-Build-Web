Before You Build

Building a PC is not just about choosing expensive components. It's about understanding your needs, setting a realistic budget, and selecting the right hardware for your purpose.

Before purchasing any components, consider how you plan to use your PC, what performance you expect, and how much you are willing to spend. A well-planned PC can save you money while delivering the performance you actually need.

Set Your Budget

Decide how much you are willing to spend before choosing components. Your budget should include all essential parts, such as the processor, graphics card, motherboard, RAM, storage, power supply, and cabinet.

Don't spend all your money on one component. Maintain a balance between performance, compatibility, and future upgrades.

If you use your PC for programming, video editing, graphic designing, 3D modelling, or professional applications, focus on processing power, sufficient RAM, and fast storage.

Components to focus on:

  • CPU: A powerful multi-core processor for demanding workloads.
  • RAM: 16GB–32GB or more, depending on your applications.
  • Storage: Fast SSD for applications and project files.
  • GPU: Important for video editing, rendering, and GPU-intensive applications.

If you're a student, you probably need a PC for studying, programming, online classes, research, and everyday productivity.

Components to focus on:

  • CPU: A good mid-range processor for multitasking.
  • RAM: 16GB is a good starting point.
  • Storage: 512GB SSD or more for software and files..
  • GPU: Integrated graphics are sufficient for basic programming and everyday tasks.

If gaming is your primary purpose, prioritize components that deliver smooth gameplay, good graphics, and stable performance.

Components to focus on:

  • CPU: A capable processor that won't bottleneck your graphics card.
  • RAM: 16GB as a starting point; 32GB for demanding games and multitasking.
  • Storage: SSD for faster game loading and reduced loading times.
  • GPU: One of the most important components for gaming performance.

If you mainly browse the internet, watch videos, use office applications, and perform everyday tasks, you don't need an expensive high-performance PC.

Components to focus on:

  • CPU: An entry-level or mid-range processor.
  • RAM: 8GB minimum; 16GB recommended for smoother multitasking.
  • Storage: 256GB–512GB SSD for everyday usage.
  • GPU: Integrated graphics are usually sufficient.
